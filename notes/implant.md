# Implant

## Working clinical concept

Adaptive Salience Restoration (ASR)

The public or patient nickname may be simpler and less clinical. "Boredom treatment" is a possibility.

## Origin

Originally developed for severe, treatment-resistant anhedonia, not for boredom and not primarily for addiction.

## Mechanism

A bilateral invasive closed-loop stimulation system with sensing and stimulation across circuits involved in:

- reward valuation
- salience attribution
- cognitive control
- motivated attention

The device continuously estimates an engagement state and applies patterned stimulation when endogenous salience is weak but present.

## What it does not do

- It does not induce euphoria.
- It does not make everything equally pleasurable.
- It does not create meaning ex nihilo.
- It does not simply block cravings.

## What successful treatment feels like

Ordinary experience becomes easier to remain with.

A patient can notice details in a tree, stay inside a conversation, fold laundry, wait in traffic, or sit through an uneventful evening without the same aversive pressure to change state.

## Philosophically interesting side effect

Patients may become less motivated to leave situations.

A boring job remains interesting enough.

A stale relationship still contains endless detail.

Waiting becomes tolerable.

The patient can still reason about goals and values, but the internal signal that says "enough, do something else" is weakened.

This keeps the technology morally ambiguous even when it works exactly as intended.
