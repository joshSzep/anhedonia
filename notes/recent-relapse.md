# Most Recent Relapse

The relapse should be ordinary rather than catastrophic.

He has several months sober. His routines are intact. Work is fine. His marriage is intact. He attends meetings. Everyone around him has reasons to believe he is doing well.

Then he gets an empty Saturday night.

Nothing happens.

That is the trigger.

He is not overwhelmed by a specific drug craving. He is increasingly unable to tolerate the fact that the next several hours contain nothing he wants.

He tries passive distractions and abandons them. He walks. He checks messages. He returns home. Ordinary consciousness begins to feel intolerable.

He obtains a short-acting substance that produces a dramatic state change.

The cycle becomes mechanical:

1. anticipation
2. rupture
3. return
4. disappointment
5. repeat

The drug no longer gives him what he imagines he wants. What he is compulsively seeking is the **transition between states**.

Later he drinks because the category "sober" already feels broken.

The next day he makes a second decision. He tries another intoxicant as an experiment, telling himself he wants to learn whether he still enjoys intoxication generally or whether the previous night was substance-specific.

The experiment goes badly. He becomes too intoxicated, loses the day, and wakes with regret.

Nothing spectacular happens externally.

That is the point.

The revelation is:

**I did not want drugs. I wanted the world to become worth paying attention to.**

Later, when a clinician describes the implant as increasing spontaneous engagement with low-salience environments, he immediately understands what it means.

They have built a machine for the empty Saturday night.
