# Premise

## Working title

**Anhedonia**

## Genre

Philosophical literary hard science fiction.

## Central speculative technology

An invasive closed-loop neural implant developed for treatment-resistant anhedonia associated with substance use disorder.

The device monitors neural activity related to salience, reward, and cognitive control, then applies adaptive stimulation when the patient enters a low-engagement state.

It does **not** manufacture pleasure or euphoria.

It amplifies weak endogenous salience signals that are already present, making ordinary stimuli more difficult for the brain to classify as irrelevant.

A cup of coffee is not suddenly ecstatic. It is simply worth attending to.

A conversation is not euphoric. The patient remains present in it.

A quiet evening does not become exciting. It becomes sufficient.

## Hard constraint

The implant cannot create salience from nothing. It can only amplify signals the nervous system is already producing.

## Philosophical problem

Boredom may not merely be a defect or unpleasant state. It may function as negative feedback that pushes consciousness to search its environment, revise goals, or leave unrewarding situations.

If boredom can be technologically removed, the treatment may also remove some of the pressure that produces exploration, change, ambition, and escape.

The device therefore raises a central question:

**Is boredom information?**

And for the protagonist:

**If I need a machine to make life feel worth inhabiting, have I recovered, or have I found a more sophisticated drug?**
