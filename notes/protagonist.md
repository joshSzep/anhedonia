# Protagonist

## Current sketch

- Early 40s
- Highly competent, educated, and externally functional
- Technically literate enough to understand the implant research
- Works in a field involving complex systems, feedback loops, or adaptive infrastructure
- Married or in a long-term relationship
- Addiction history spans more than one substance
- The common denominator is not a particular chemical, but **state alteration**

He is not primarily trying to destroy himself. He is trying to make consciousness feel different.

His recovery has made him more stable and more functional, but not obviously happier.

His private heresy is:

**What if recovery is succeeding at the wrong objective function?**

## Strength

Genuinely curious about other people and capable of tenderness.

## Liability

He intellectualizes pain. He can convert an emotional truth into a theory before he has to feel it.

## Core conflict

He can dismantle shallow objections to the implant:

- "It's artificial." So are psychiatric medications.
- "It changes the brain." So do therapy, meditation, trauma, learning, and addiction.
- "Then the recovery isn't real." Why?

The objections he cannot easily dismantle are personal:

- If the implant makes him content, does the contentment count?
- If it changes what he wants, who is doing the wanting?
- If it makes his marriage easier, is that love or modulation?
- If he becomes easier to love after surgery, was everyone right to prefer the modified version of him?
